# Special Engineering Alloys Enhancement Plan

Prepared for: Rubinox Metal and Alloys  
Target page: `/materials/other-metals.html`  
Benchmark pages: SMO 254, Titanium, Duplex & Super Duplex, Nickel Alloys  
Target score: 92+

## Benchmark

The successful SMO 254, Titanium, Duplex and Nickel implementations all follow a clear pattern:

- Convert a short category page into a buyer-focused technical landing page.
- Preserve the Rubinox visual style and one-H1 page structure.
- Add practical comparison tables and application sections.
- Add visible FAQs before FAQPage schema.
- Add buyer specification guidance and an RFQ checklist.
- Use cautious, procurement-safe language instead of unsupported claims.
- Strengthen internal links to relevant material pages and conversion routes.

Special Engineering Alloys should use the same structure while solving a slightly different problem: the page covers broad, uncommon and specification-led enquiries, so it must help buyers define the requirement clearly enough for Rubinox to review.

## Objective

Reposition `/materials/other-metals.html` from a broad "other metals" page into a strong "Special Engineering Alloys" buyer landing page.

The page should help buyers answer:

- Which alloy families can be reviewed under special engineering alloys?
- What details are needed when a material is uncommon, trade-named or project-specific?
- How should buyers specify alloy steel, tool steel, spring steel, cobalt alloys, zinc, lead, tin, specialty bronze or hard-to-source grades?
- Which product forms and processing requirements should be included in the enquiry?
- When should buyers use the main material category pages instead?
- What documents, testing, inspection and delivery details should be included in the RFQ?

## Current Gaps

Current strengths:

- Meta title and meta description are present.
- One clear H1 is present.
- WebPage and BreadcrumbList schema are present.
- Page already explains that exact grade/specification matters.
- Internal links to main material ranges are present.
- CTA section exists with WhatsApp, email RFQ and quotation guide.

Gaps to close:

- Page theme is still broad and named "Other Ferrous & Non-Ferrous Metals" rather than a stronger "Special Engineering Alloys" position.
- Word count and topical depth are light for a category page.
- Visible FAQ section is short.
- FAQPage schema is missing.
- Comparison/table coverage is missing.
- Buyer specification guide is missing.
- RFQ checklist is missing.
- Product forms and processing guidance can be expanded.
- Internal linking can be made more contextual.
- A visible encoding issue appears in the availability note: the quoted "Other metals" text renders as mojibake and should be corrected during implementation.

## Recommended Page Additions

### 1. Page Positioning Update

Purpose:

- Move from a vague "Other Metals" page to a stronger high-intent category page.
- Capture searches around special engineering alloys, specialty metals, hard-to-source metal grades and specification-based supply.

Recommended positioning:

- Page H1: `Special Engineering Alloys Supplier in India`
- Supporting copy: Rubinox reviews enquiries for special engineering alloys, ferrous and non-ferrous specialty metals, hard-to-source grades, trade-name materials and project-specific metal requirements based on exact grade, standard, form, dimensions, quantity, testing and delivery location.

Guardrails:

- Keep one H1.
- Do not imply every alloy is always available.
- Do not promise stock or instant supply.
- Use "review enquiries", "can support enquiries", and "subject to grade, form, size and source feasibility" language.

### 2. Special Engineering Alloy Families Section

Purpose:

- Give buyers a clear map of what belongs on the page.
- Improve topical relevance without making unsupported availability claims.

Recommended table:

| Alloy family | Examples to mention | Common buyer intent | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Alloy steels | EN series, AISI alloy steels, heat-resistant steels | Strength, wear, heat treatment, machinery and fabrication | Specify standard, grade, condition and mechanical property requirement |
| Tool steels | D2, H13, M2, O1 or project-specified grades | Dies, tooling, cutting, forming, wear-resistant parts | Specify hardness, heat treatment, size and machining requirement |
| Spring steels | EN45, EN47, 65Mn or equivalent grades | Springs, clips, washers, flexible components | Specify temper, hardness, thickness, width and application |
| Bearing steels | 52100 / 100Cr6 or equivalent grades | Bearings, rollers, balls, shafts and wear parts | Specify cleanliness, hardness, heat treatment and tolerance |
| Cobalt-based alloys | Stellite-type or project-specified grades | Wear, heat and corrosion-resistant components | Avoid brand/trademark claims unless buyer specifies grade and source route |
| Specialty non-ferrous metals | Zinc, lead, tin, specialty bronze, cobalt, tungsten or molybdenum enquiries | Electrical, wear, balance weight, lining, thermal or chemical use | Grade, purity, composition and form are essential |

### 3. Ferrous vs Non-Ferrous Specialty Metal Comparison

Purpose:

- Clarify buyer language and help procurement teams place the enquiry correctly.

Recommended table:

| Category | What it usually means | Typical enquiry examples | Buyer details needed |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ferrous specialty metals | Iron-based grades beyond standard mild/carbon/stainless categories | Alloy steel, tool steel, bearing steel, spring steel, wear-resistant steel | Grade, standard, heat treatment, mechanical properties and form |
| Non-ferrous specialty metals | Metals not based on iron, often selected for corrosion, conductivity, weight, wear or special properties | Specialty bronze, zinc, lead, tin, cobalt alloys, copper alloys and rare non-ferrous grades | Composition, purity, temper, dimensions, quantity and application |
| Trade-name materials | Materials requested by a brand or trade name | Cobalt alloys, nickel alloys, engineering alloys or project-specified trade names | Generic grade, UNS/EN/ASTM reference, equivalent permission and source requirements |

Guardrails:

- Do not claim trade-name equivalence automatically.
- Recommend buyers share the exact datasheet, drawing or project specification.

### 4. Product Forms and Processing Matrix

Purpose:

- Improve RFQ clarity and lead quality.
- Mirror the successful matrix pattern used on Titanium, Duplex and Nickel.

Recommended table:

| Requirement type | Common forms | Processing details to define |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Flat products | Sheets, plates, strips, coils, flats, shims | Thickness, width, length, temper, finish, tolerance and cutting |
| Long products | Round bars, rods, hex bars, square bars, wires, profiles | Diameter, length, straightness, condition, hardness and machining allowance |
| Tubular products | Pipes, tubes, hollow bars | OD, ID, wall thickness, schedule, length, standard and testing |
| Forged/cast items | Forgings, castings, blocks, rings, discs | Drawing, heat treatment, machining, NDT and inspection |
| Finished components | Fasteners, washers, machined parts, custom items | Drawing, tolerances, surface finish, quantity, inspection and packing |

### 5. Application Sections

Purpose:

- Add buyer intent beyond a generic material list.
- Help industrial buyers see how to frame requirements.

Recommended subsections:

#### Wear and Abrasion Applications

Cover:

- Wear plates.
- Liners.
- Bushes.
- Shafts.
- Rollers.
- Dies and tooling.
- Bearing and sliding components.

Buyer details:

- Wear mode, hardness, impact load, surface finish and drawing.

#### Heat and High-Temperature Applications

Cover:

- Heat treatment fixtures.
- Furnace components.
- High-temperature fasteners.
- Tooling and thermal processing parts.

Buyer details:

- Operating temperature, atmosphere, thermal cycling, load and grade/standard.

#### Electrical and Conductivity Applications

Cover:

- Copper alloys, specialty bronze, tin, zinc and other conductive material enquiries.
- Connectors, contacts, bus components and electrical hardware where specifications define the grade.

Buyer details:

- Conductivity requirement, temper, dimensions, finish and inspection.

#### Chemical and Corrosion-Resistant Applications

Cover:

- Specialty metals for chemical, marine, process, plating or corrosion-related service.
- Route buyers to Nickel Alloys, SMO 254, Duplex, Titanium, Copper and Brass pages when those pages are more specific.

Buyer details:

- Media, concentration, temperature, pressure, pH, chloride level and standard.

### 6. Standards, Trade Names and Equivalents Guidance

Purpose:

- Help buyers avoid unclear enquiries.
- Add useful AEO/SEO content around equivalent grades and trade names.

Recommended content:

- Explain that buyers should provide at least one of:
  - ASTM/ASME standard.
  - EN/DIN/JIS/IS standard.
  - UNS number.
  - AISI/SAE/EN grade.
  - Manufacturer datasheet.
  - Chemical composition.
  - Drawing or approved material list.
- Explain that trade names may need generic grade confirmation.
- Explain that equivalents should be approved by the buyer or project engineer.

Guardrails:

- Do not promise exact equivalent substitution.
- Do not claim certifications or approvals.
- Do not list proprietary grades as if Rubinox owns or certifies them.

### 7. Buyer Specification Guide

Purpose:

- Convert broad research traffic into actionable RFQs.
- Reduce back-and-forth for uncommon grades.

Recommended section title:

`Special Engineering Alloys Buyer Specification Guide`

Recommended table:

| Requirement | What buyer should specify |
| --- | --- |
| Material name | Exact alloy, trade name, generic grade or chemical composition |
| Standard or reference | ASTM, ASME, EN, DIN, JIS, IS, UNS, AISI, SAE or project specification |
| Product form | Sheet, plate, strip, coil, bar, rod, wire, tube, pipe, forging, casting, fastener or machined part |
| Dimensions | Thickness, width, length, OD, ID, wall, diameter, tolerance or drawing |
| Condition | Annealed, hardened, tempered, normalized, hot rolled, cold drawn, solution treated or buyer-specified |
| Mechanical properties | Hardness, tensile strength, yield strength, impact, elongation or other project requirement |
| Testing and documents | MTC, PMI, chemical analysis, hardness, UT, MPI, DP, dimensional inspection or third-party inspection |
| Application | Wear, heat, electrical, chemical, marine, tooling, fabrication or custom component use |
| Quantity and delivery | Quantity, unit, delivery city, export details, packing, timeline and urgency |

### 8. RFQ Checklist

Purpose:

- Improve conversion quality without adding pricing or stock claims.

Recommended checklist:

- Material name, grade or trade name.
- Generic grade, UNS or equivalent designation if known.
- ASTM/EN/DIN/JIS/IS/project standard.
- Product form and complete dimensions.
- Required condition, temper, hardness or heat treatment.
- Quantity and unit of measure.
- Application or service environment.
- Testing, inspection and certificate requirements.
- Drawing, datasheet or approved material list.
- Delivery location, packing and timeline.
- Whether equivalent grades are acceptable.

CTA notes:

- Primary CTA: WhatsApp quote.
- Secondary CTA: Email RFQ.
- Tertiary CTA: Quotation guide/page.
- Keep existing Rubinox conversion pattern.
