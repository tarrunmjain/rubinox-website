SS 304 vs SS 316 vs SS 316L

SS 304, SS 316 and SS 316L are among the most common stainless steel grades. The right choice depends on corrosion exposure, chloride level, welding, fabrication, project standard and end-use environment.

GradeCommon buyer reasonCorrosion positioningCommon product formsBuyer note
SS 304General-purpose stainless steel for fabrication, equipment and structural use.Suitable for many indoor, food, architectural and general industrial uses.Sheets, plates, pipes, tubes, coils, bars, flanges and fittings.Review where chloride exposure is limited and project specifications allow 304.
SS 316Molybdenum-bearing stainless steel for stronger corrosion resistance than 304.Often reviewed for marine, chemical and chloride-bearing service.Pipes, tubes, sheets, plates, bars, flanges, fittings and fasteners.Review when the buyer requires better pitting resistance than 304.
SS 316LLow-carbon version of 316 for welded fabrication and corrosion-sensitive service.Similar corrosion positioning to 316 with low-carbon chemistry for weld-related requirements.Sheets, plates, pipes, tubes, coils, fittings, flanges and fabrication items.Review when welding, low carbon or project specification calls for 316L.

SS 310 vs SS 321 vs SS 347 for Heat Service

Heat-resistant stainless steel enquiries should define operating temperature, atmosphere, pressure, load, fabrication route and the required project standard before grade selection is finalized.

SS 321 / 321HTitanium-stabilized stainless steel.Often reviewed for welded high-temperature service and intergranular corrosion resistance.Pipes, tubes, sheets, plates, bars, fittings and welded components.Review when stabilized stainless is specified for heat-affected fabrication.
SS 347 / 347HNiobium-stabilized stainless steel.Often reviewed for high-temperature welded components and pressure/process applications.Pipes, tubes, plates, bars, flanges, fittings and pressure components.Review when 347/347H is specified for high-temperature process or pressure service.

Stainless Steel FAQs

What stainless steel grades does Rubinox supply?

Rubinox handles enquiries for stainless steel grades including 201, 202, 304, 304L, 316, 316L, 310, 321, 347, 904L, Duplex, Super Duplex, 17-4 PH, Nitronic 60 and SMO 254 based on buyer specification.

What is the difference between SS 304, SS 316 and SS 316L?

SS 304 is a general-purpose stainless grade, SS 316 includes molybdenum for stronger chloride and chemical resistance, and SS 316L is the low-carbon version often reviewed for welded fabrication or low-carbon project requirements.

When should buyers choose SS 316L instead of SS 316?

Buyers often review SS 316L when welding, low carbon chemistry, corrosion-sensitive fabrication or project specifications require the low-carbon version of SS 316.

Is SS 304 suitable for outdoor or marine use?

SS 304 can be suitable for many general outdoor uses, but marine, coastal or chloride-bearing service often requires review of SS 316, SS 316L, Duplex, SMO 254 or other project-specified grades.

Which stainless steel grade is used for high-temperature service?

SS 310, SS 310S, SS 321, SS 321H, SS 347 and SS 347H are commonly reviewed for heat-related service. The right grade depends on temperature, atmosphere, pressure, fabrication and project standard.

What is the difference between SS 310, SS 321 and SS 347?

SS 310 is often reviewed for heat and oxidation resistance, SS 321 is titanium-stabilized for welded high-temperature service, and SS 347 is niobium-stabilized for high-temperature process or pressure applications.

Which stainless steel product forms can be requested?

Buyers can request pipes, tubes, sheets, perforated sheets, plates, coils, flanges, fittings, fasteners, flats, angles, channels, rods, bars, circles and wire mesh according to grade, size, standard and quantity.

What details are needed for a stainless steel quotation?

Share grade, product form, standard, dimensions, quantity, finish, testing requirements, application, delivery location and timeline. Drawings or datasheets are helpful for fabricated or custom items.

Can Rubinox review MTC or inspection requirements?

Rubinox can review enquiries where MTC, PMI, hydrotest, ultrasonic testing, hardness testing, IGC, dimensional inspection or third-party inspection is required. Buyers should state these requirements at enquiry stage.

When should buyers review Duplex, SMO 254, Titanium or Nickel Alloys?

Buyers should review Duplex, SMO 254, Titanium or Nickel Alloys when the project requires higher strength, severe chloride resistance, seawater service, high-temperature performance or a non-standard alloy specification.
