Nickel 200 vs Nickel 201

Nickel 200 and Nickel 201 are commercially pure nickel grades used for caustic handling, chemical processing, electrical components and fabricated parts. Nickel 201 is the low-carbon version and is commonly reviewed when elevated-temperature exposure makes carbon control important.

High-Temperature Applications

Nickel alloys are often reviewed for high-temperature service where oxidation resistance, thermal stability, creep strength or strength retention is part of the buyer specification.

  • Furnace components and heat treatment fixtures
  • High-temperature fasteners, bars and fabricated parts
  • Thermal processing equipment and expansion components
  • High-temperature pipes, tubes, sheets, plates and rods

Corrosion-Resistant Applications

Nickel alloy suitability depends on media, concentration, temperature, pH, flow rate, deposits, crevices, welding and maintenance conditions.

  • Acid handling and chemical environments
  • Chloride-bearing process media and brine exposure
  • Scrubber systems and corrosion-resistant piping
  • Flanges, fittings, fasteners, plates, sheets, tubes and bars

Chemical Processing Applications

Chemical processing buyers often evaluate nickel alloys for reactors, vessels, heat exchangers, acid handling lines, scrubber components, process piping, flanges, fittings and fasteners. RFQs should mention chemical media, concentration, operating temperature, pressure, product form, ASTM/UNS requirement, MTC needs and any drawing or datasheet.

Marine Applications

Marine and offshore buyers often review Monel 400, Monel K-500 and Inconel 625 for seawater piping, pump and valve components, shafts, fasteners, marine hardware and brine systems. Final material selection should consider flow, stagnant water, crevice risk, temperature and chloride concentration.

Heat Exchanger Applications

Nickel alloys are commonly requested for heat exchanger tubes, condenser tubes, tube sheets, plates, baffles and shell-and-tube process equipment. Buyers should specify tube OD, thickness, length, plate dimensions, UNS grade, ASTM reference, MTC requirement, testing such as hydrotest, ultrasonic or eddy-current inspection if required, and the process media or end-use environment.

Nickel Alloy FAQs

What are nickel alloys used for?

Nickel alloys are used for chemical processing, high-temperature equipment, marine service, heat exchangers, corrosion-resistant piping, flanges, fittings, fasteners, bars, sheets and plates where the buyer specification requires nickel-based performance.

What is the difference between Inconel, Monel and Hastelloy?

Inconel, Monel and Hastelloy are nickel alloy families with different chemistry and service strengths. Inconel is often reviewed for heat, strength and oxidation resistance, Monel for nickel-copper marine and brine service, and Hastelloy for severe chemical corrosion depending on the exact grade.

Which nickel alloy is suitable for high-temperature applications?

High-temperature selection depends on temperature, atmosphere, stress, fabrication and standard. Buyers commonly review grades such as Inconel 600, Inconel 718, Incoloy 800 and Hastelloy X when high-temperature service is specified.

Which nickel alloy is reviewed for marine or seawater service?

Monel 400, Monel K-500 and Inconel 625 are often reviewed for marine or seawater-related requirements, while final selection depends on flow, temperature, chloride level, crevice risk, strength and the project specification.

What is the difference between Nickel 200 and Nickel 201?

Nickel 200 is commercially pure nickel, while Nickel 201 is the low-carbon version. Nickel 201 is often reviewed where lower carbon is required, especially for elevated-temperature exposure.

Which ASTM standards should buyers mention for nickel alloy products?

ASTM references depend on grade and product form. Buyers should mention the required UNS number, ASTM or ASME standard, product form, size, quantity, testing, MTC requirement and end use in the RFQ.

What information is needed for a nickel alloy quotation?

A useful nickel alloy RFQ should include grade, UNS number if specified, standard, product form, dimensions, quantity, finish or condition, testing, documentation, application and delivery location.

Can Rubinox supply nickel alloy pipes, tubes, plates, bars, flanges and fittings?

Rubinox handles enquiries for nickel alloy pipes, tubes, sheets, plates, coils, bars, rods, flanges, fittings, fasteners and fabricated components according to buyer grade, standard, size and documentation requirements.

Are nickel alloys better than stainless steel or duplex stainless steel?

Nickel alloys are not always better than stainless steel or duplex stainless steel. The right material depends on corrosion media, temperature, strength, fabrication, standards, lifecycle requirements and project constraints set by the buyer or project engineer.

Does Rubinox provide MTC or test certificates for nickel alloy materials?

Rubinox can support buyer requests for MTC and testing documentation where required. Buyers should specify certificate, inspection and testing requirements clearly in the RFQ.
