Why SMO 254 Is Used for Chloride-Rich Service

SMO 254, UNS S31254 and 6Mo stainless steel are commonly selected when chloride exposure creates a higher risk of pitting, crevice corrosion and localized attack. It is considered for seawater, brackish water, coastal plants, chemical processing and high-chloride process streams where standard stainless grades may need stronger corrosion resistance.

Grade selection should always consider chloride concentration, temperature, pH, flow rate, crevice geometry, cleaning chemicals, fabrication method and project standards. 316L vs 904L vs SMO 254: Which Grade Should You Choose?

Grade Common Use Case Corrosion Positioning Buyer Notes
316L General stainless steel service, fabrication, food, pharmaceutical and moderate chloride environments. Useful for many applications, but may be limited where chloride pitting risk is high. Often reviewed first when service conditions are moderate and project standards allow it.
904L Chemical, acidic and marine-adjacent environments requiring higher alloy content than standard stainless grades. Higher corrosion resistance than many standard austenitic stainless steels in selected environments. Can be suitable where specification, fabrication and commercial requirements match the service.
SMO 254 / UNS S31254 Seawater, desalination, brine handling, scrubbers, offshore, marine and chloride-rich process systems. Commonly considered for demanding chloride service where pitting and crevice corrosion resistance are priorities. Best selected after confirming temperature, chlorides, design, standards, testing and documentation needs.

SMO 254 is not automatically the right grade for every project. The better selection depends on service conditions, design life, fabrication needs, inspection requirements and the governing material specification.

Desalination and Seawater Applications

SMO 254 is commonly specified in desalination and seawater projects because these systems combine chlorides, deposits, crevices, elevated temperatures and continuous operation. It may be reviewed for:

  • Seawater intake, outfall and transfer piping
  • Reverse osmosis and brine handling systems
  • Evaporator, condenser and heat exchanger components
  • Coastal utilities, marine equipment and offshore packages
  • High-chloride chemical and scrubber service

SMO 254 Supply FAQs

What is SMO 254 stainless steel?

SMO 254 is a high-alloy austenitic stainless steel also known as UNS S31254 or 6Mo stainless steel. It is commonly considered for chloride-rich, seawater, chemical, marine and desalination service where standard stainless grades may need higher pitting and crevice corrosion resistance.

Is SMO 254 the same as UNS S31254?

SMO 254 is commonly associated with UNS S31254 and 6Mo stainless steel. Buyers should confirm the required standard, material specification, product form, testing and project documentation before placing an order.

When should buyers consider SMO 254 instead of 316L?

Buyers often consider SMO 254 when 316L may not provide enough resistance for chloride-rich, seawater, brackish water or high-pitting-risk service. Final grade selection depends on operating conditions, concentration, temperature, design and project specifications.

How does SMO 254 compare with 904L?

904L offers stronger corrosion resistance than many standard stainless grades, while SMO 254 is often selected for more demanding chloride and seawater environments. The right choice depends on service conditions, fabrication needs, standards, testing and commercial requirements.
